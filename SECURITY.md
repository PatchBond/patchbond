# Security

PatchBond holds other people's money and accuses people of wasting time, so both the contract and
the judge are built to fail safe: **no method in the current code lets us take a held bond or a
vault, and when in doubt the contributor is refunded.** We do hold the upgrade key and the NEAR AI
key registry on testnet — every power we keep, and every money path with what it rests on, and the attacks we ran against them, are in
[docs/TRUST.md](docs/TRUST.md). This page states what each key can and cannot do, the protections in place, and the
known limits — honestly.

Report a vulnerability privately to the maintainers via a GitHub security advisory on
[PatchBond/patchbond](https://github.com/PatchBond/patchbond/security/advisories/new). Please
don't open a public issue.

## Who can do what

| Key | Can | Cannot |
|---|---|---|
| **Repo payout account** (the maintainer, or their DAO) | Withdraw its own vault; change its bond policy; refund any deposit at once; *propose* a forfeit (48 h, disputable for free, re-judged); hand the repo to another account (`set_payout`) | Touch other repos; forfeit a bond on its own say-so; overrule an AI refund without a staked challenge; decide a dispute or a challenge it raised; shorten the 48 h dispute window |
| **Oracle** (our server) | Register repos; link an **unlinked** repo after GitHub sign-in + a NEP-413 wallet proof; propose verdicts; resolve challenges; mark reviews; pay bounties to merged fixes | Withdraw any vault; relink a repo that already has a payout account; move funds to itself |
| **Owner** | Rotate oracle and treasury; withdraw the 10% treasury balance to the treasury account; pause **new** deposits | Touch vaults, deposits, bounties, budgets or claims; block refunds or withdrawals |
| **Agent function-call key** | Call `deposit_from_budget` on the PatchBond contract, within the budget's daily cap | Attach tokens; call any other method or contract; withdraw or raise its own budget |
| **Anyone** | Pay a bond; finalize a verdict whose challenge window passed; stake a challenge (payer on a forfeit, maintainer on a refund) | Fake a deposit (`ft_on_transfer` only accepts the configured USDC contract) |

**Limits of the agent's key.** NEAR enforces the key's scope: one contract, one method, no attached deposit, and a NEAR allowance that only pays gas. The contract enforces the budget: daily cap, most per bond, allowed repos, freeze. When the allowance runs out the key stops working, and the owner adds a new one; the budget isn't touched. The agent's account should keep a full-access key held by its owner, never by the agent: an account with only function-call keys can't add keys or transfer NEAR, so an exhausted allowance would lock it out. The sandbox demo's last section shows a prompt-injected agent refused five ways (`bash scripts/demo-fast.sh`).

## Protections (each has a test)

- **Vault isolation** — only the linked payout account withdraws; owner and oracle are refused. ([money_paths.rs](contract/vault/tests/money_paths.rs))
- **Link once** — a stolen oracle key can't redirect a linked vault; only the current payout account hands it over. (`a_stolen_oracle_key_cannot_redirect_a_linked_vault`)
- **Linking needs two proofs** — GitHub admin rights (re-checked at the moment of linking) and a NEP-413 signature by a *full-access* key of the payout account; challenges are single-use and expire in 10 minutes; OAuth is CSRF-protected. ([maintainers.test.ts](server/test/maintainers.test.ts))
- **No lost or created money** — every payout's callback books the amount back (vault, treasury, budget or claimable) if the transfer fails; book-keeping happens before transfers. The split of every settlement is a pure function checked on 50,000 random cases; the testnet e2e checks conservation across all accounts. ([settle.rs](contract/vault/src/settle.rs), [testnet report](docs/e2e/testnet-report.md))
- **Pause** — the owner can stop new bonds, challenges and budgets during an incident; refunds, settlements, withdrawals and claims keep working. (`pause_stops_new_money_but_never_refunds`)
- **Storage griefing** — every stored record is paid for by real money: bonds (≥ the repo's minimum), challenges (= the bond) and new budgets (≥ 1 USDC). (`a_penny_budget_is_refused`)
- **Replaying a signed forfeit** — every route accepts each signature once. On the oracle route the signed request must also name this repo and PR, and the contract checks that; on the enclave route it has only hashes, so the binding comes from the pinned wasm ([docs/TRUST.md](docs/TRUST.md#what-the-outlayer-callbacks-verify)). (Found by our own adversarial review on 2026-11-01; `a_forfeit_genuinely_signed_by_near_ai_settles_on_chain`, and the demo shows the replay refused.)
- **Griefing the price** — each payer counts at most 2 toward the queue everyone else's bond is priced on; their further open deposits raise only their own next bond, so one account can't flood the queue to price out newcomers. Many funded accounts can still each add 2. (`a_flood_from_one_payer_does_not_raise_the_price_for_everyone_else`, [queue.rs](contract/vault/src/queue.rs))
- **Agent spending policy** — on top of the daily cap, the budget's owner sets the most per bond, the repos the agent may pay into, and a freeze; only the owner can change them. (`the_owners_policy_limits_what_the_agent_can_pay_for`)
- **Griefing a PR** — a deposit paid for someone else's PR is voided (full refund, no reputation effect) and the real author can still pay.
- **Agent keys** — the MCP server checks the key on-chain and refuses full-access keys or keys that can call anything but `deposit_from_budget`. ([mcp.test.ts](mcp/test/mcp.test.ts))
- **The judge can't forfeit on its own** — an AI verdict forfeits only with confidence ≥ the repo's threshold, concrete evidence and a hard fact (failing CI, phantom code, a duplicate or injection). The exception is a disputed maintainer forfeit: a person already decided, so the re-judge needs the threshold and evidence but no hard fact. These rules stop a false forfeit from the rules' side; they don't prove the model can't be misled by what it reads. Contributor text is fenced as untrusted, our tags are neutralised, and output must match a strict schema. A failed lookup is never an accusation. ([judge.test.ts](server/test/judge.test.ts))
- **Optimistic verdicts** — AI verdicts stand only after a challenge window; either side can stake to force a re-judge.
- **Relayer** (gasless bonds) — forwards exactly one thing: USDC `ft_transfer_call` into PatchBond with a bond or challenge msg, 1 yocto, ≤150 Tgas, one per account per 10 s, and at most `RELAYER_DAILY_NEAR` (default 2 NEAR) of gas per UTC day across all senders; anything else is refused before it spends gas. ([relayer.test.ts](server/test/relayer.test.ts), testnet e2e stage 7b)
- **Webhooks** are HMAC-verified; forged deliveries are rejected. ([bot.test.ts](server/test/bot.test.ts))

## Trust assumptions and known limits

- **The oracle is trusted for GitHub facts** (who is an admin, PR state, CI results), because a contract can't read GitHub. A compromised oracle could propose wrong *refunds* — mitigated by the challenge window and the public record — but it **cannot forfeit** without NEAR AI's TEE-signed response saying so (checked on-chain in `contract/vault/src/tee.rs` against the enclave keys in NEAR AI's attestation report, registered by the owner), the signed request must name this repo (`set_repo_name`: oracle once, then only the payout account) and this PR, and each signature is accepted once — so a genuine forfeit can't be replayed against an honest PR. It cannot withdraw or redirect a linked vault. **What it can still do:** on the oracle route it chooses the request, so it could feed the model a fabricated diff under the right PR name and get a genuine forfeit. Every request is published (detectable by anyone comparing it with GitHub), and the OutLayer route with `exclusive` on closes it — `exclusive` is on for the live testnet contract (since 1 Nov), so there the oracle can't forfeit at all.
- **Sealed reports** — the plaintext exists in the reporter's browser, inside the OutLayer enclave and NEAR AI's TEE while judging, and with the maintainer. Our server stores ciphertext only. The enclave's key is derived from a secret OutLayer generated inside TDX.
- **The AI verdict is independently verifiable** — the TEE signature over the exact bytes is checked in the browser. The contract recovers it too (`ecrecover`), but what that proves differs by route: on the oracle route it reads the verdict from the signed bytes (`tee.rs`); on the enclave route it sees only hashes, so the verdict itself rests on OutLayer's attested worker ([docs/TRUST.md](docs/TRUST.md#what-the-outlayer-callbacks-verify)). A signature shows who answered, not that the answer is right. A maintainer's forfeit carries no signature: it's a 48 h proposal, and **a contributor who doesn't dispute within 48 h loses the bond**. The bot posts a PR comment when the forfeit is proposed (amount, UTC deadline, how to dispute), so they hear about it on GitHub.
- **Bounties without the oracle** — `claim_bounty_in_tee` pays an issue's bounty only when the OutLayer enclave confirms on GitHub that the fix is merged into this repo, claims the issue, and is bound to the recipient (a `PatchBond: <account>` line in the PR description, which only the author and repo admins can edit). It doesn't check that the change really fixes the issue: the maintainer's merge counts as the fix. With `exclusive` on, the oracle's `pay_bounty` is refused. Trusted: OutLayer's attested worker and GitHub (no model is involved, so there's no NEAR AI signature). Tests: `contract/vault/tests/bounty.rs`, `outlayer/judge/tests/bounty.rs`.
- **Removing the oracle from judging** — with OutLayer configured, `judge_in_tee` runs the whole judge (GitHub fetch, facts, prompt, NEAR AI call, rules) in an Intel TDX enclave, and with `exclusive` the oracle can't forfeit at all (the live testnet config has `exclusive` **on** since 1 Nov: forfeits come only from the enclave, and a disputed verdict is re-judged there too — anyone calls `rejudge_in_tee`, the enclave sends the stronger model the PR plus both sides' on-chain statements, and the contract settles on NEAR AI's signature). What's then trusted is OutLayer's attested worker — for the verdict, not only the inputs — and its worker registration (TDX quotes checked on-chain against an allowlist its admins control), GitHub, and NEAR AI's enclave keys. See [docs/OUTLAYER.md](docs/OUTLAYER.md).
- **Upgrades** — the contract account's key (held by the team) can redeploy the code. That is normal for a hackathon deployment and is how the v0.1 → v0.2 `migrate` ran on testnet. For a production mainnet deployment the plan is to move upgrades behind a DAO with a time lock, or remove the key.
- **Storage** — the contract pays NEAR for storage out of its own balance; the floors above make griefing it cost more than it does.
- **Farming the "proven" exemption (open).** An account with 3 refunds on 2 repos and no forfeit in 90 days pays no bond anywhere. Someone who controls two GitHub repos can earn that cheaply: install the App on both and link them, open 3 trivial PRs that name their own NEAR account, pay the bonds, and refund them as each repo's maintainer. With `exclusive` on, the refund counts only if the enclave read that account in the PR, but the attacker writes the PR, so that doesn't stop them. Cost: gas, OutLayer runs (0.002–0.004 NEAR each) and the bonds while they're held, which come back. The bound: the exemption ends with the account's first forfeit, and after that its bonds cost up to 4× for 90 days; each new account has to farm again; and every bond-free PR is still judged and public. No repo can opt out of the global exemption yet.
- **The dispute statement window is enforced by our server, not the contract (open).** When a verdict is disputed, our bot waits `REJUDGE_DELAY_MIN` (20 minutes) for both sides to post statements, or less if both already have, before it calls `rejudge_in_tee`. But the contract lets anyone call `rejudge_in_tee` as soon as a deposit is disputed, so a challenger who calls it directly can have the stronger model re-judge before the other side has posted. What it can't do: change the rules the re-judge applies, the model, or what the enclave reads from GitHub. The fix is a contract-side earliest-start time per dispute (or both sides' statements), planned after the hackathon.
- **Reputation** is per NEAR account; a determined spammer can make new accounts. Paying the bond on a stranger's good PR no longer takes the refund credit: a refund counts only if the PR itself names the payer (see [TRUST.md](docs/TRUST.md#screening-fixes-whats-live)). Planned: count refunds only from repos with an outside, linked maintainer, and let repos opt out of the global exemption. That's why the bond, not reputation, is the defence: every new account pays the full, queue-priced bond.
- Not audited. It is hackathon software: read the code and the tests.
